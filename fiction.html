The Gabbard Gazzate!

The Journey to the Days Unborn Monday “Good morrow, diary, the queerest things are astir. This morn a gilded shovel fell from the heavens.[1] I touched it and was whisked away into the middle of a bustling village. The paths roared lounder than any dragon and more crowded than jousting day. Mine eyes were blinded by flickering torches as I was surrounded by shining yellow horseless carriages. Tall towers that reached the sky, made from oddly smooth stones filled my view.. An abandoned scroll lied on the street, “Some careless herald must’ve dropped it,” I muttered.[2] Upon opening, it had no calligraphy nor illuminations, except a large title proclaiming “The New York Times” but the date was all the more flumexing, “June 30th 2026.” Alas I've been transported into the days unborn, but perchance the gilded shovel shall reappear. Tuesday “Prithee tell me young lad, where am I?” I questioned a small boy, who was trotting along beside me. “You’re in Queens, of course! Duh!” Replied the young boy, garbed in a loose tunic with a picture of a man with a flowing red cloak. “Is that your lord, on your tunic?” “No, that's Superman, don’t you know who he is? Besides, what's a tunic, this is a T-shirt!” laughed the child as he dashed off to an enormous bright yellow horseless carriage filled with young lads and lasses.[3] Quickly I trod as I searched the sky for this Queen’s castle, perhaps their sovereign would undo this sorcery and place me in mine own proper time. Gabbard 24 Wednesday “Whrrr” a metal bird flew above me as I ducked and covered mine ears.[6] Yet no others did so. “Hey there, do you know where the Renaissance Fair is?” asked a knightly looking man dressed in shining armor. “No good sir, I too am a stranger, can you take me to your soverign?” quoth me. “What sovereign? Do you mean the mayor? This is New York, not the middle ages!” replied the man in astonishment. “But isn’t this not Queens, where is her castle?” flumoxed, I replied. “Queen Elizabeth’s castle was in England, but she’s been dead for like 500 years anyway.” retorted the man. All the more lost I wandered around this “New” York, what was wrong with the old one, I wondered. I had been to York multiple times, but it looked nothing like this. Heavy-hearted I wander still. Thursday This forenoon I wandered to the tower called the Empire’s building, once again I was disappointed, no sovereign here. This here tower is quite barren of decoration, yet full of townsfolk. No sovereign reigns, yet peace remains, what is this land? Today I tarried little and journeyed to the Statue of Liberty. This green lass here is the height of my lord’s tallest tower. I looked across the sea, wondering what was there. “England, Europe, Asia, all lay that way” replied a young man whom I quesitoned. “Yonder lay my homeland, when shall I see you again” is my lonely cry. Gabbard 24 Friday Home again, back to my country, back to my time! This final morn in “New” York, I went to the English tower.. I met the ambassador’s assistant, he spoke with an accent quite like mine. “Go to the airport and get a plane, I’m not going to get any private jet for you!” Unfortunately he did not understand my origin in this country. On my way out of this disappointing tower I saw a sign for “Central Park, Let’s Garden!” I walked in and there on the ground laid the golden shovel, taking a breath I picked it up. “Whoosh” I landed right back where 5 days ago I had picked up the shovel, the only proof I had left was thee dear diary. Nancy, mine wife, was loudly yelling for supper, as if nothing had happened. I’ll never forget, nor understand, this flumexing journey to the days unborn.
